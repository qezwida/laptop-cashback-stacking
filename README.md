## The fine print that kills cash back (read this before you checkout)

This is the part most deal coverage skips, and it's where real money gets lost. These terms come straight from TopCashback's own retailer pages.

**The coupon code trap.** On Dell Refurbished, using a coupon code that isn't displayed on TopCashback voids your cash back. So if you're about to enter a code you found on a deal forum, you're choosing between the code's discount and the cash back — do the math both ways before deciding. On a $411 refurbished laptop, 5% back is about $20; a code would need to save you less than that to be the worse option.

**Safari tracking failures.** TopCashback explicitly states it cannot guarantee cash back tracking on Safari for certain retailers, including Best Buy and Dell Refurbished. The fix costs nothing: place the order in Chrome, Edge, or Firefox.

**Cash back excludes tax, shipping, and fees.** The percentage applies to the item price only. On a $1,600 gaming laptop, the 12%-category math applies to $1,600, not the inflated post-tax total. Plan your expectations accordingly.

**The claim windows differ by retailer — and they're short.** If cash back doesn't track automatically, Best Buy gives you 30 days from the order to file a Missing Cash Back Claim. Dell Refurbished gives you 100 days. Miss the window and the money is simply gone, so it's worth checking your TopCashback account a few days after any laptop order rather than assuming it worked.

**Bulk and resale purchases are ineligible.** Buying multiple units with resale intent can get the cash back declined outright. For a personal laptop purchase this is irrelevant, but it's in the terms.

**Initial tracking can show a lower amount.** TopCashback notes that cash back may first track at a lower figure and correct upward once the purchase is approved. Don't panic at the first number you see; do follow up if it never corrects.
